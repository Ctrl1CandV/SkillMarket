# Method Sources and Trade-offs

## University reading methods and open scholarly metadata (2026-10-12)

This section draws on public primary-method guidance and data documentation. It adopts reusable reading, verification, and information-organization practices. Course quotas, institutional reputation, and database scores do not replace review of paper content.

| Source | What this plugin adopts | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Waterloo, S. Keshav, [How to Read a Paper, UCSD-hosted course copy](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~dstefan/cse227-spring22/papers/keshav%3Ahow.pdf) | Locate the question in stages, read mechanisms and evidence, reconstruct the argument; expand candidates from recent related work, references, and formal proceedings | The three-pass approach deepens reading as needed; it does not require a full close reading of every paper or treat a reasoned reconstruction as an experiment replication. |
| [Stanford CS224U project guidance](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/projects.html) | Compare work around a shared question; explain disagreements, limitations, and future questions; preserve bibliographic details accurately | Do not copy the course's fixed paper count, page count, or assignment format. A synthesis of several papers is not the same as reading a review paper. |
| [CCF recommended-list, seventh edition, and notes](https://www.ccf.org.cn/Academic_Evaluation/By_category/) | Look up the actual field, journal/conference, and publication track. Released 2026-03-31, with an erratum in April; use the version available at the time of future checks. | Conference tiers in the list refer to full/regular papers; do not apply them to other tracks. Venue tier is not a quality certification for an individual paper. |
| [OpenAlex Works attributes](https://help.openalex.org/data/works/attributes/) | Record the source/date for `cited_by_count`; interpret `fwci`/normalized percentile by type, year, and field; check DOI, year, open version, and known retraction markers | Use only fields actually returned; record missing values as unknown. Normalization affects comparisons, not correctness. Follow current API/access requirements and do not estimate unavailable values. |
| [Crossref REST API](https://www.crossref.org/documentation/retrieve-metadata/rest-api/) | Verify DOI and publication metadata; cross-check publication details and updates | Metadata depends on publisher deposits. An entry alone does not prove that the full text was checked or peer review is complete. |
| [dblp official open-metadata note](https://blog.dblp.org/2026/04/29/results-and-findings-from-the-2025-2026-dblp-user-survey/) | Supplementary bibliographic checks of computer-science titles, authors, venues, and version relationships | Do not treat dblp as a citation ranking. Check publication/acceptance sources for the specific record. The FAQ could not be accessed in this review, so it was not checked in full. |
| [Original DORA declaration](https://sfdora.org/read/) | Continue with content review after metadata screening; avoid using venue metrics as a substitute for assessing an individual work | Do not erase explicit user requirements for venue tier or citations. Separate the purpose of screening from content-quality judgment. |

The common screening windows, scenario-specific attribute priorities, source/maturity labels, and compression reconciliation checklist above are this plugin's design, synthesized from these methods and user requirements. They are not a universal ranking algorithm prescribed by universities. Top venues and the last three years are a common starting point; foundational classics emphasize fundamentals and lasting influence, while timely applications emphasize recency and matching settings. Do not relax hard requirements for a specific task. Recheck dynamic citation counts, venue lists, and publication status for every paper-selection task. This review of method documents does not verify any particular candidate.
