# de-ai-flavor

Make the intended message clear and easy to read. The goal is to improve expression, not to eliminate every supposed “AI trace” or turn all writing into casual speech or short sentences.

## What does “AI-sounding” mean here?

“AI-sounding” refers to observable writing problems: saying a lot without providing specifics, describing ordinary actions awkwardly, repeating the same paragraph structure, using a tone that does not fit the purpose, or leaving readers to infer how sentences relate. Human writing can have these problems too; they do not show whether text was generated by AI.

Identify the specific sentence or passage and explain its effect on the reader before choosing a revision. A particular word, em dash, three-item list, or formal tone is not inherently a problem. Keep expressions that already work.

| Common issue | How to recognize it | How to improve it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vague or overstated claims | The wording could describe any product or topic; it says only that something is important, comprehensive, or efficient | State functions, actions, causes, or conditions supported by the material. Narrow the claim when evidence is missing; do not invent details. |
| Awkward or unnatural phrasing | Simple actions are buried under layers of nouns; verbs and objects do not fit; references are unclear | Say who does what, improve word pairings, and split the sentence or name the object when needed. |
| Mechanical structure or repetition | Every paragraph announces a point and then lists three items; headings, body, and conclusion repeat the same message | Organize around the reader's questions, merge synonymous repetition, and retain distinct reasons and necessary steps. |
| Unclear relationships | Short labels are piled together; conclusions skip their reasoning; metaphors are harder to understand than the concepts | Add necessary cause, conditions, and transitions. Use accurate terms and explain them for the reader's level. |
| Tone does not fit the situation | An ordinary notice sounds like an advertisement, a report like a speech, or a button like a slogan | Adjust tone to the purpose. Courtesy can remain; praise, sentimentality, and slogans need not be added. |
| Production notes mixed into finished copy | A page says “this module is newly added” or an article says “here is the optimized version” | Keep finished copy focused on what its readers need; put revision notes in the delivery message. |

## Drafting from scratch

1. Determine the reader, purpose, and message from the task. There is no need to find the original author or request a sample before choosing an ordinary tone.
2. Arrange known information in a useful order. A notice starts with the event and action; instructions explain steps and conditions; an article develops ideas and reasons. Do not default to “background–significance–three points–outlook.”
3. Write specific content before polishing sentences. Match the length to the material. Develop a longer piece with explanations, reasoning, and relevant examples instead of synonymous repetition. Ask a question only when missing information would affect the conclusion.
4. Review the draft using the checks below. Do not add experiences, emotions, rhetorical questions, jokes, or deliberate mistakes just to sound human.

## Revising existing text

1. Read the whole text and confirm its meaning and delivery requirements. If a draft or sample is provided, retain the parts of its tone, judgment, and habits that suit the task. Without such material, write for the audience and situation instead of assuming a particular author's voice.
2. Find the issues that most affect readability. Fix content order and repetition before adjusting sentences and word choice. Do not merely substitute synonyms or turn a list of empty phrases into one long empty sentence.
3. Preserve useful explanations, arguments, and examples. Concision does not mean reducing everything to a summary: short labels suit buttons and column headings; complete sentences suit body text and spoken scripts that need explanation.
4. Compare the revision with the original. Leave natural sentences as they are; do not force uniform sentence length, punctuation, paragraph shape, or personal style.

## Change the wording, preserve the meaning

Keep facts, numbers, proper names, negation, conditions, strength of evidence, and attribution intact. Do not turn “may” into “will” or a goal into an achievement. Do not invent experiences, data, or product capabilities for vividness. Preserve quotations, code, formulas, links, and fixed copy as required by the task; change only natural-language text that is in scope. When fiction is explicitly requested, invent freely but do not present it as fact.

## Read once before delivery

- Can readers follow what is being said, who it helps, and what they need to know or do?
- Are vague judgments, repeated ideas, awkward phrasing, or missing explanations still present? Would removing them lose distinct information?
- Did any claim become stronger, condition disappear, or unsupported promise get added in the name of naturalness?
- Do the tone, length, and format fit the purpose? Keep technical terms, necessary qualifications, useful summaries, and lists when they help.

By default, deliver text the user can use directly. List specific issues and reasons when the user asks for an editorial review. Do not provide an “AI flavor score” or claim that all AI-sounding language has been eliminated. Stop when the text is clear, natural, and fit for purpose; do not rewrite it again in a different template.
